## 1. Method and confidence

The PDF has a Tamil text layer, but extraction exhibits character and spacing corruption. Extracted text was used for navigation and broad reading. Selected pages containing high-impact details were checked against rendered page images, including the land-sale instrument (p. 161), the engagement ring (p. 164), the recovered-property sequence (pp. 176–177), and the three deaths (p. 191), with further page-image checks around the oath / disclosure sequence (pp. 188, 192–193).

Use these distinctions throughout the study:

- **SOURCE OBSERVATION:** what the text directly states or depicts.
- **CHARACTER REPORT / BELIEF:** what a character says, remembers, suspects or believes.
- **ANALYSIS:** an interpretation of how the material functions.
- **OPEN:** a question the supplied pages do not resolve.
- **PROVISIONAL NAME / RELATIONSHIP:** spelling or identity requires confirmation because the text layer is unreliable or the relationship is not yet explicit.

Do not treat a character's supernatural explanation as an independently verified cause. Do not turn a mystery into canon for KARUPPI. This is literary research only.

## 4. Source-derived observations by sequence

### 4.1 The old property, grandmother and wet traces — Chapters 9–10, pp. 82–101

- Rajendran explains to Reena that he has brought her to a property connected with his father's last days.
- He frames the investigation around the possibility that events could have human causes or something beyond ordinary human action.
- The property is reached by an overgrown / neglected route and has distinctive guardian-like architectural features.
- Reena sees a strange green creature in the vegetation.
- An old portrait of a woman identified in the dialogue as Rajendran's grandmother is visible in the house.
- The earlier checkpoint notes record wet traces / footprints moving through the property and ending at a mound identified as the grandmother's samadhi.
- A sound is interpreted by a character as oppari. Its actual source is not established in this checkpoint.
- The wet traces are physical observations within the narrative; their origin is OPEN.

**Questions:** What is the grandmother's full history? Is she the same person as any of the women in the separate village thread? What is the relationship between the old house, the samadhi and the father's final days? Do not merge these identities without direct text support.

### 4.2 Karuppu's marriage pronouncement — Chapters 11–12, pp. 102–117

- The village ritual sequence presents Karuppu speaking through a possessed / ritual medium.
- The deity's announcement identifies Rajendran, son of Aanaiyudaiyan, as a groom for Selvi, associated with Sankili Muniyandi's family.
- The priest / ritual figure collapses after the pronouncement.
- The families respond with shock, resistance and discussion rather than uncomplicated acceptance.
- The announcement places Rajendran's family directly inside the village's Karuppu-based marriage authority.

**Belief boundary:** the text depicts a ritual pronouncement and the community's response. It does not independently establish the source of the voice or the supernatural mechanism.

## 9. Research-only craft observations

These are analytical notes, not story proposals or canon:

- **Institutional folklore:** Karuppu's shrine functions as a religious authority and a community mechanism for testing accusations and vows.
- **Material power:** land, irrigation water, jewellery and documentary control drive concrete human conflict.
- **Belief as social force:** characters make marriage, property and safety decisions partly in response to what they believe Karuppu will do.
- **Unreliable information channels:** Rajendran's account is explicitly challenged; family memory, village report and personal experience must be separated.
- **Object-based evidence:** the ring, land deed, jewellery and wet traces have different evidentiary roles. Do not merge their chains.
- **Ambiguous causality:** extraordinary weather, possession, the mysterious stranger and shrine punishment must remain distinct from human acts unless the novel itself links them.
- **Honour and silence:** Pichchamma's reluctance to disclose information creates a conflict between social reputation and truth.
