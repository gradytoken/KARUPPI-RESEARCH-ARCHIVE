## 2. Chapter 34 and final sequence — PDF pp. 285–298

### 2.1 The hidden jewellery and Sivakami's explanation — pp. 285–290

- The final chapter opens with the hidden valuables / jewellery becoming visible and the group confronting the chain of concealment.
- The dialogue revisits who hid the gold pot, how its hiding place became known, and why it was moved or reburied near the samadhi.
- Sivakami explains her role in the concealment and in maintaining the appearance of the old woman's haunting.
- When directly challenged about the Pechi Paati ghost disguise, **Sivakami admits that she wore the disguise**.
- She describes a concealed / secret room and a method of changing appearance and disappearing from view when people approached the property.
- The earlier supposed ghost appearances are thereby given a human mechanism: costume / wig, access to a secret space, foreknowledge of visitors, and deliberate staging.
- Her account connects the disguise to the protection and concealment of valuables and to the fear that someone might discover the jewellery.
- The dialogue also revisits past incidents that were staged to frighten or deter people. Do not convert every event in the story into a human trick merely because this one haunting is exposed as a disguise.

**Conclusion:** Pechi Paati's apparent ghost was, in this strand, a deliberate human impersonation by Sivakami. This is not proof that every Karuppu manifestation or every uncanny event has the same explanation.

### 2.2 The safari-suit body is identified — pp. 291–293

Rina explains the earlier safari-suit body and the trap around it.

- The man in the safari suit was a **CBI police officer** who had come to investigate / observe the events.
- Kasi killed him without initially realising that the victim was a CBI officer.
- The body was deliberately left / used in the exposed rocky location as bait, with the expectation that Kasi would react or come to the scene.
- Rina and Aravind had prepared a counter-operation and hidden themselves / arranged for others to be in position.
- This resolves the earlier open identity question: the safari-suit victim was not an anonymous corpse or a supernatural apparition. He was an investigator killed by Kasi.
- Rina also explains the clue trail by which she identified Kasi and anticipated his movements. The postal / letter information and the Ceylon connection form part of this investigative reasoning.

**Status:** source-derived explanation given by Rina. The CBI officer's death and the lure operation are part of the novel's human-investigation resolution.

### 2.3 Kasi exposed as the human Karuppu impersonator — pp. 273–275, 294–295

The final confrontation confirms the limits of Rina's earlier hypothesis.

- Rina had already identified Kasi as Parandama Nayakkar's son and proposed that he was the person acting under / as Karuppu.
- In the final encounter, the horse-mounted Karuppu figure admits that he wore the Karuppu disguise.
- He justifies his actions as punishment of wrongdoers, arguing that punishing sinners is the deity's work.
- Rina explicitly tells him that he is not Karuppusamy but the ordinary human Kasi.
- Kasi reveals that he has used the identity / authority to create fear and control the village. His explanation ties this to revenge against those who wronged his family, as well as his continuing desire for the hidden gold / jewellery.
- Even when challenged, he is reluctant to abandon the mask because he believes fear is necessary to restrain injustice and wrongdoing.
- The text does not endorse his claim to be the deity. It presents him as a man who has assumed Karuppu's appearance and authority.

**Conclusion:** Kasi is the human impersonator. His motive combines revenge, punishment, control through fear and the pursuit of the hidden valuables. His own moral justification does not make his impersonation the genuine deity.

### 2.4 The genuine Karuppu intervention — pp. 295–297

- As the confrontation reaches its climax, a violent storm / lightning event accompanies an extraordinary decapitation sequence.
- Witnesses later describe the intervention as Karuppusamy's arrival. They say they had mistaken the figure for Kasi, but insist that it was the deity.
- The witness account describes the figure raising the aruval and cutting down the person who had been threatening them.
- The assembled villagers interpret the event as a genuine act of Karuppu's justice, not merely another staged appearance by Kasi.
- The sequence therefore preserves a real supernatural / divine presence within the novel, even after the human impersonation is exposed.
- Rina and the others also encounter a mysterious helper at the shrine, who frees / revives them and then leaves.

**Evidence boundary:** the novel gives the witnesses' and villagers' interpretation of the intervention as Karuppu. The extraordinary manifestation is narrated with deliberate supernatural force. Do not collapse this into Kasi's impersonation.

### 2.5 The sandalwood-scented stranger and teacher Varadarajan — pp. 296–298

This is the final identity link that must be preserved carefully.

- After the climactic event, a mysterious man appears at / near the shrine and frees Rina, Aravind and the others from their predicament.
- Aravind recognises him as the same person who had earlier helped him when he was injured and given him medicine.
- This reconnects the final scene to the recurring large-moustached / sandalwood-scented stranger described in the earlier Rajendran and Aravind accounts.
- The man leaves before the crowd can fully question him.
- The final page then shows Shanmugam grieving beside a headless body.
- Devar identifies the dead man as **teacher Varadarajan**, Shanmugam's father, and says that he died after doing a good deed.

**Strong narrative inference:** the mysterious helper / recurring stranger is linked to teacher Varadarajan, whose death is disclosed at the very end. The ending asks the reader to reinterpret the previous healing and rescue encounters in light of Varadarajan's sacrifice and the supernatural protection surrounding Karuppu.

**Do not overstate:** the final pages identify Varadarajan's body and connect it to the helper's intervention, but they do not reduce every aspect of Karuppu to a simple one-line identity equation. Keep separate:
1. Kasi, the human who impersonated Karuppu.
2. Karuppu, the genuine protective / punitive presence acknowledged by the final witnesses.
3. Varadarajan, the teacher whose mysterious appearances / assistance culminate in a sacrificial death and whose identity is disclosed by the closing scene.

This layered distinction is the core of the ending.

### 2.6 Closing resolutions — p. 298

- The final explanation acknowledges the lovers' relationship: Aravind and Rina, and Shanmugam and Lakshmi, are accepted / affirmed.
- The hidden gold pot and jewellery are addressed in the closing exchange, with instructions / intention to restore or return the valuables rather than leave the concealment unresolved.
- The group reacts to the extraordinary helper and the events as something beyond an ordinary human encounter.
- Shanmugam remains beside Varadarajan's body; Devar consoles him and asks him to stand up and face what must come next.
- The novel ends with the teacher's sacrifice remembered as a good deed.

## 5. Final chronology outline

The narrative's ordering is non-linear. This outline records the major causal sequence reconstructed from the full audit; dates should only be assigned where the source gives them.

1. Earlier family / village history establishes the Nayakkar property, the gold / jewellery and the injustice that later fuels Kasi's grievance.
2. The 1975 childhood episode establishes Rajendran's encounter with a mysterious moustached, sandalwood-scented stranger.
3. The village's Karuppu authority becomes entangled with marriage, vows, punishment, land, water and criminal activity.
4. Mokkamaayan's gang and Brahma's operation contribute to theft, hostage-taking and the wider property conflict.
5. Parandama's land document is deceptively handled; the Nayakkar family's losses and deaths deepen the grievance.
6. The Pechi Paati ghost appearances are staged by Sivakami, who uses disguise and a secret route / room.
7. Kasi, Parandama's son, assumes Karuppu's identity and uses religious fear as a tool of revenge and social control.
8. The CBI officer investigates and is killed by Kasi; the safari-suit body is later used as bait.
9. Rina's independent investigation connects the human evidence, identifies Kasi and challenges the village's supernatural assumptions.
10. Rina tests the marriage prohibition by arranging Lakshmi and Shanmugam's garland exchange at the shrine.
11. Rina and Aravind find the hidden valuables / samadhi evidence; Rajendran is attacked; the group prepares to draw Kasi out.
12. Kasi appears in Karuppu disguise and is confronted. The climax is accompanied by an extraordinary intervention attributed by witnesses to genuine Karuppu.
13. The mysterious helper frees the investigators. Aravind recognises him from the earlier healing encounter.
14. The final scene identifies teacher Varadarajan, Shanmugam's father, as the man who died doing a good deed. The lovers' relationship and the remaining valuables are addressed in the closing resolution.

## 7. What makes the ending structurally effective — ANALYSIS

- **Two-layer reveal:** the apparent supernatural omnipresence has a human impersonator (Kasi), but exposing the imposture does not erase the genuine supernatural presence. The novel avoids the simplistic “everything was fake” solution.
- **A separate ghost mystery:** Sivakami's Pechi Paati disguise resolves one haunting without explaining away the other uncanny encounters.
- **Clue payoff:** the safari suit, the hidden terrain, the gold pot, the old inventory, the sandalwood scent and the recurring stranger all become meaningful in the final stretch.
- **Moral distinction:** Kasi claims to punish sinners, but his revenge and manipulation are not identical to genuine justice.
- **Character-driven investigation:** Rina's test of the marriage taboo is not just dialogue; it is an action that changes her own fear and gives her the resolve to continue.
- **Sacrificial closure:** Varadarajan's final identification reframes the earlier mysterious assistance as a morally significant act rather than an arbitrary miracle.
