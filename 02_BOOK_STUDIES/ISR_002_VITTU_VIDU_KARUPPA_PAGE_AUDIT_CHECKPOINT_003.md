## 3. Source-derived observations

### 3.1 Safari-suit body, lure and village reaction — Chapter 30 continuation, pp. 256–262

- The body in the distinctive safari suit, first introduced at p. 255, remains central to the events at the rocky location.
- Dialogue indicates that the body / its placement is being used to draw out whoever is responsible. The working expectation is that Kasi may come or react when the body is seen.
- Rina and Aravind observe events while trying not to reveal their own presence / plan.
- The villagers' fear and speculation spread quickly.
- A ritual / possession episode follows. The figure identified by the villagers as Karuppu gives instructions concerning a concealed direction / location in the northern forest area.
- Rina challenges the group about deaths being attributed to Karuppu and insists that the responsible human actor must be found.

**Important ambiguity:** the following pages introduce a striking description of the apparent safari-suit corpse coming to life. Do not treat the original body's identity, status or the exact mechanics of the scene as settled. The sequence may involve staging, mistaken identification or another device, but the precise explanation must come from the remaining pages.

### 3.2 The shrine / village confrontation continues — Chapters 30–31, pp. 259–264

- A priest or ritual medium enters a state that the villagers interpret as Karuppu's arrival.
- The voice / figure gives directions towards a hidden location and speaks of a person who has been watching / observing the village.
- Rina questions the credibility and purpose of these claims.
- The elders and villagers debate whether the investigation should proceed and whether the deity's instructions must be obeyed.
- Rina's position is clear: the deaths and threats require an accountable explanation, and the religious identity invoked by the perpetrators must not end the inquiry.

**Belief boundary:** possession and divine speech are reported / depicted ritual events. Whether the voice is supernatural, humanly produced or manipulated remains open.

### 3.3 Hidden terrain and surveillance / vantage-point lead — Chapters 31–32, pp. 265–270

- Rina and Aravind examine a rocky, difficult-to-access area.
- The terrain contains concealed passages / recesses and positions from which people can watch routes or activity around the shrine.
- The narrative suggests that the person behind the events could observe people without being easily seen.
- The safari-suit body and the rocky terrain are linked by the continuing investigation, but the exact identity and staging of the body remain unresolved.
- The villagers continue searching for the figure / location identified through the shrine sequence.

**Analysis:** the novel is shifting the mystery from an apparently omnipresent deity to a potentially human system of concealment, observation and staged appearances. This is a hypothesis supported by the terrain and dialogue, not yet a complete explanation.

### 3.4 Kasi identified by Rina — Chapter 32, especially pp. 273–276

The rendered text on p. 273 was checked.

- Rina says Kasi is not an unknown outsider: she identifies him as the son of Parandama Nayakkar, whose family was unjustly ruined / wronged by the people being addressed.
- In the dialogue, Rina says, in effect, “he is Karuppusamy.”
- She immediately qualifies the statement: she cannot say whether Kasi came to believe he was Karuppusamy or deliberately put on Karuppu's disguise / identity.
- Rina argues that Kasi has a grievance and a possible motive for revenge, and that the actions attributed to Karuppu may be his work.
- Her argument is based on motive, opportunity and the connections between the old property / gold-pot / Nayakkar history. It is a strong investigative theory, not yet a fully demonstrated proof in this page range.
- The people listening react with surprise and resistance; not everyone accepts Rina's conclusion immediately.

**Critical wording boundary:** “Kasi is Karuppu” is Rina's identification of the human figure / assumed identity behind the Karuppu appearances. It must not be restated as “Kasi is the actual deity” or as a settled supernatural revelation.

### 3.5 Rina's reconstruction of the gold-pot trail — pp. 274–276

- Rina says that the apparent omniscience of the Mai / oracle figure can be explained through human observation, information access and manipulation.
- The previous search for the gold pot and the disturbed samadhi / burial site become part of her reasoning.
- She suggests that the pot's hiding place and the people who knew of it can expose the person who has been using the Karuppu identity.
- The elders question how the hidden pot could be located. Rina's prior inspection of the burial site becomes relevant.
- The exact route from the recovered pot / missing jewellery to Kasi's current hiding place remains to be established.

### 3.6 Aravind's stranger account creates a contradiction — pp. 277–278

- Aravind explains that he secretly returned to the village after his knife injury and visited the Karuppu shrine while seeking help.
- He recounts that a large, moustached, physically imposing stranger found him unconscious / injured, helped him recover, attended to the wound and was associated with a strong sandalwood fragrance.
- Rina asks whether this stranger could be Kasi.
- Aravind rejects the identification as unlikely because the stranger appeared much younger than the age he estimates Kasi would now be. Kasi would be over about 45, whereas the stranger looked far younger (Aravind's estimate is approximate).
- Rina continues to distinguish the possibility of genuine divine help from the human impersonation / revenge theory.

**Continuity consequence:** do not collapse the moustached stranger from Rajendran's childhood and Aravind's injury account into Kasi. A meaningful age-description contradiction now exists. The identity of the stranger remains OPEN.

### 3.7 Lakshmi and Shanmugam's secret marriage — Chapter 33, pp. 282–284

The rendered pages 282–284 were checked.

- Rina reveals that, during an earlier visit, she discovered that Lakshmi and Shanmugam (the teacher's son) were in love.
- Lakshmi was not afraid of Karuppu's supposed opposition to their relationship and was prepared to marry Shanmugam even if the village / deity condemned it.
- Rina arranged for the two to exchange garlands / marry at the Karuppu shrine itself.
- This was a deliberate test of the village's belief that Karuppu would punish people who went against his marriage pronouncements.
- Rina's reasoning is that a genuinely just Karuppu would not oppose true love. She wanted to see whether any punishment or divine response would follow.
- The absence of the predicted punishment changed Rina's own fear and accelerated her investigation.
- Rina says she did not leave when others advised her to do so; she stayed close to Rajendran and continued investigating the mystery of Pechi Paati's supposed ghost / appearance.

**Analytical significance:** Rina is not merely a sceptical listener. She has conducted a controlled, risky test inside the community's own sacred space, using a real couple's relationship to test a supernatural claim. This is a source-derived action and a major character revelation.

**Boundary:** the test weakens the village's specific claim that Karuppu opposes this love / secretly married couple. It does not prove that every event attributed to Karuppu is humanly fabricated.

## 6. Updated hypotheses (not conclusions)

1. **Human impersonation / revenge:** Kasi, son of Parandama Nayakkar, may be the person behind at least some appearances and actions attributed to Karuppu.
2. **Controlled observation:** concealed rocky terrain may allow someone to monitor temple / village movements and stage apparently impossible appearances.
3. **Information manipulation:** the apparent omniscience of the oracle / Mai may be explainable through surveillance, social information and deception.
4. **Separate genuine or unexplained events:** the moustached stranger's healing actions do not fit Rina's current Kasi theory on age grounds. The novel may be preserving a distinct supernatural or human thread.
5. **Rina's active investigation:** the secret marriage test demonstrates that Rina has deliberately tested, rather than merely debated, the village's supernatural claims.

## 8. Research-only craft observations

- **A real test, not a debate:** Rina uses the shrine itself as the site of an experiment. She does not rely only on argument.
- **Mystery reframing:** a supposedly omniscient deity may be a human actor exploiting surveillance, village information and fear.
- **Competing anomalies:** the stranger's apparent healing does not fit the Kasi identity hypothesis. The novel preserves a second unexplained thread while the human conspiracy becomes clearer.
- **Setting as mechanism:** rocky terrain, concealed passages, sightlines and the shrine's location become part of how apparent omnipresence can be manufactured.
- **Character agency:** Rina emerges as an active investigator with her own plan and risk tolerance, rather than a passive companion to Rajendran.
