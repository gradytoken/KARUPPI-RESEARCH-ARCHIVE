## 3. Source-derived observations

### 3.1 Amaravathi's warning and Rina's refusal to stop — Chapter 26, pp. 227–232

- Amaravathi warns Rajendran and Rina not to oppose or challenge matters attributed to Karuppu.
- Her position is that the deity's commands should be accepted and acted upon.
- Rina questions the logic of accepting every claim without examining what is actually happening.
- Rina resists being frightened out of the investigation and indicates that she wants to see for herself whether the events are caused by a person or by the deity.
- The exchange reinforces the difference between inherited communal belief and evidence-seeking inquiry.

**Status:** Amaravathi's warning and Rina's response are explicit dialogue. The deity's agency remains a belief / question, not an established causal fact.

### 3.2 Rina's discovery of a written valuables record; Rajendran attacked — Chapter 27, pp. 233–238

- Rina and Rajendran enter and search the old property.
- Rina examines an old written record / inventory containing detailed entries for valuables, including gold and diamond ornaments, necklaces, rings and other jewellery.
- The record appears to preserve specific information about a substantial collection. Its full ownership trail is not yet established.
- While Rina is reading / investigating, Rajendran is no longer beside her.
- She hears a sound, searches for him and finds him on the ground, seriously wounded.
- A large aruval is visible beside him, with blood at the scene.
- Rina seeks help. The attacker is not identified in the pages reviewed.

**Analysis:** the attack occurs during the investigation of the old property's secrets. Timing makes the investigation a plausible motive, but does not prove who attacked Rajendran or why.

### 3.3 Rajendran's medical crisis and blood donation — Chapter 28, pp. 239–246

- Rajendran is taken for medical care after the aruval injury.
- The narrative records his recovery from a life-threatening condition.
- Ratna thanks Aravind for donating blood to her brother Rajendran.
- Rina separately questions Aravind about the donation; the conversation identifies the blood group as AB negative and describes it as rare.
- Do not merge Ratna with Rina. They are distinct names / characters in the text.
- A related conversation discusses Aravind's earlier knife injury and a visit to the Karuppu shrine while he was still recovering.
- Aravind's account says that a large-moustached stranger appeared, attended to the wound / gave treatment and spoke to him. This resembles young Rajendran's 1975 stranger encounter from the prior checkpoint.
- Rina states that she believes in and respects Karuppasamy but does not accept wrongdoing carried out in the deity's name.

**Belief boundary:** Aravind's encounter is a character account. Its healing / supernatural interpretation remains open. The similarity to Rajendran's childhood encounter is a pattern worth tracking, not proof that both strangers were the same entity.

### 3.4 Pullaanguzhal Pechi's disturbed samadhi — Chapter 29, pp. 247–249

- Rina and Aravind inspect the old property and its grounds.
- They notice that a samadhi / burial site associated in the conversation with the elderly woman identified as **Pullaanguzhal Pechi** has been disturbed / dug into.
- They excavate further and uncover metal vessels.
- The excavation also exposes a human skull and small bone fragments.
- One vessel is identified as a brass pot associated with the storage of gold and diamond jewellery belonging to Ratna's grandmother.
- The jewellery itself is not present in that pot.
- The text discusses the possibility that the ornaments have been removed and concealed elsewhere.
- A photograph inside the property is identified as Pullaanguzhal Pechi. Confirm in later pages whether this person is definitively the same individual as Ratna's grandmother and the elderly woman in the earlier jewellery / lake sequence; the connection is strongly suggested but should not be made more definite than the dialogue allows.

**Key distinction:** the vessel and human remains are physical discoveries in the narrative. The ownership, movement and current location of the missing jewellery remain unresolved.

### 3.5 Rina's human-conspiracy hypothesis and the name Kasi — Chapter 29, pp. 250–251

- Rina and Aravind discuss whether a person or group may be using Karuppu's name as cover for revenge.
- Rina proposes that people connected with the wronged Nayakkar family may be behind some of the acts attributed to Karuppu.
- A person named **Kasi** is introduced as a significant lead.
- Dialogue associates Kasi with the Nayakkar family and refers to a period / connection involving Ceylon.
- Rina's working idea is that identifying Kasi could help expose the rest of the people involved.
- Aravind agrees that Kasi is a major lead.

**Status:** This is Rina's investigative hypothesis, not a solved reveal. Kasi is a person of interest, not an established killer or mastermind in this checkpoint.

## 6. Cross-thread developments

The following are investigative connections, not solved explanations:

1. **The stranger motif:** Rajendran's 1975 childhood account and Aravind's later injury account both describe a large-moustached stranger associated with help / healing. Their identity is not established.
2. **The property and jewellery:** an old written valuables record, a brass pot connected with Ratna's grandmother's jewellery, and the disturbed samadhi now form a material evidence chain. The missing ornaments remain unlocated.
3. **The human-conspiracy possibility:** Rina suspects that some alleged Karuppu punishments may be human revenge carried out under the deity's name. Kasi becomes a concrete lead.
4. **The attack on Rajendran:** the violence occurs while the old property is being investigated. It may be connected to the valuables or family secrets, but the evidence is not yet sufficient to assign motive.
5. **The safari-suit body:** the discovery at the start of Chapter 30 introduces a new possible link to the people Rina and Aravind have seen. The identity must be established first.
