## 2. Cross-study craft patterns

### A. Rules can organise a village before they explain a mystery

Across these studies, a local custom, sound, or prohibition can make the audience notice what a community considers normal. The strongest use is behavioural: people act differently because the rule exists.

### B. The village must be more than scenery

Community reactions, social status, labour, ritual roles, and competing beliefs give the setting a working structure. A village feels specific when these structures influence what people can say and do.

### C. Geography and objects should carry information

The physical landscape and objects become useful when they control access, evidence, risk, or choice. A strange prop without a consequence is decoration, not clue engineering.

### D. Witnesses and histories are partial

A folk verse, local belief, or elder's story can preserve real memory while distorting cause, motive, or chronology. A later reveal should complicate testimony fairly rather than making every earlier witness foolish.

### E. Human power can exploit supernatural belief

*Adhu Mattum Ragasiyam* makes this particularly explicit: temple fear is used to preserve a material power structure. *Ragasiyamaaga Oru Ragasiyam* also mixes human manipulation with spiritual traditions. These are not identical resolutions and should not be collapsed into one universal formula.

## 3. Comparative cautions

- The studies have unequal coverage. *Adhu Mattum Ragasiyam* has a scanned-source study, *Vittu Vidu Karuppa!* and *Ragasiyamaaga Oru Ragasiyam* have prior syntheses with page-level audits pending, and *Thangakkaadu* is not studied.
- Do not generalise about the author's entire body of work from three novels.
- Do not infer that all supernatural events in any one novel are fake or that all are genuine.
- Do not confuse similar motifs (temple, village, hidden object) with equivalent plot structures.

## 4. KARUPPI questions — not story decisions

- What ordinary behaviour in the 1990 village can make an existing mystery legible before anyone explains it?
- Which restrictions are enforced socially, and by whom?
- Which locations and objects change what the characters can know or do?
- Where can two people give honest but incompatible accounts of the same event?
- Does a reveal change a character's choices, relationships, or the community's power structure?
- Is an apparent supernatural explanation being assumed too early, or a human explanation being used to close the mystery too conveniently?

No plot device, mythology, character, or signature image from these novels is approved for copying into KARUPPI.
