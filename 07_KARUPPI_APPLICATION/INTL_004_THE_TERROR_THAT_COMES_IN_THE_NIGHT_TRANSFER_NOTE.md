# INTL-004 — The Terror That Comes in the Night: Conditional KARUPPI Transfer Note

**Source study:** `02_BOOK_STUDIES/INTL_004_The_Terror_That_Comes_in_the_Night.md`
**Source:** David J. Hufford, *The Terror That Comes in the Night: An Experience-Centered Study of Supernatural Assault Traditions* (University of Pennsylvania Press, 1982)  
**Status:** Research application only. No story canon changes approved.  
**Project boundary:** Preserve the present-day 1990 village mystery. The removed historical Story B mythology remains out of active development.

## 1. Why this book matters

Hufford's strongest contribution is a method for investigating strange reports without forcing the witness's experience into the investigator's preferred explanation.

He separates:

- the event the person reports;
- the sensations and perceptions the person describes;
- the explanation the witness believes;
- the community tradition or label available to them;
- the investigator's reconstruction and interpretation.

This is especially useful for a mystery whose investigation depends on accounts, drawings, rumours, local beliefs and incomplete records. It helps keep evidence, belief and inference from collapsing into one another.

## 2. Candidate tests against existing KARUPPI material

| Instrument | Potential research test | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Open first-person account | Ask a witness to narrate an already-established event before the investigator introduces a term or theory. | Do not add new incidents or alter existing accounts without the Master Story Bible. |
| Event / perception / interpretation ledger | Separate what happened, what the person perceived, what they inferred and what they believe caused it. | A witness's belief is not objective truth by itself. |
| Interview provenance | Mark firsthand account, hearsay, family retelling, public rumour, official record or investigator reconstruction. | Do not treat all sources as equally reliable. |
| Question-order audit | Keep broad questions before suggestions. Record when Rajan first introduces a term or theory. | Do not add evidence merely to imitate the questionnaire. |
| Negative-case comparison | Track residents who have not witnessed an event or do not accept the village explanation. | Non-experience helps assess distribution; it does not automatically disprove an event. |
| Experience-feature index | Catalogue already-established sounds, light, posture, timing, movement, visibility, memory and sensory impressions across accounts. | Do not import the Old Hag's specific symptom pattern. |
| Tradition / experience split | Track who knew a belief before an incident and who adopted it afterward. | Do not assume the belief caused the event. |
| Classification threshold | Require multiple independent features before treating two reports as the same phenomenon. | Do not link unrelated mysteries without evidence. |

## 3. Practical witness-account record

For each existing account, a research file can preserve:

1. Exact witness wording, including uncertainty and self-correction.
2. When and where the event allegedly occurred.
3. What the witness was doing immediately beforehand.
4. What they directly saw, heard, touched or smelled.
5. What they could and could not do.
6. Sequence and duration as remembered.
7. Who else was present and what those people directly observed.
8. Whether the witness knew a local explanation before the event.
9. Who supplied the explanation afterward.
10. What changed after the witness heard other accounts.
11. Which details are independently corroborated, contradicted or unknown.

This is a research instrument, not a proposal to add scenes or characters.

## 5. What not to transfer

Do not import the Old Hag, Newfoundland hagging, witch-riding, incubus or succubus, vampire, Inuit augumangia / ukomiarik, aswang, sleep paralysis, out-of-body experience, or any of the book's protective practices as KARUPPI mythology. These examples have their own historical and cultural contexts.

Do not use the book to diagnose any KARUPPI character or claim that an existing strange event has a sleep-related or medical cause. This is a method for researching testimony, not a medical evaluation.

Do not revive the removed historical Karuppi / Maari Story B, the Ravi-Selvaraj triangle, the counter-spell or magical-ring lineage.

Use *The Terror That Comes in the Night* as an **evidence-provenance and witness-interview reference**. The next useful research exercise, when the latest Master Story Bible is the active source, is to audit existing witness accounts and clues with the event / perception / interpretation ledger above.
