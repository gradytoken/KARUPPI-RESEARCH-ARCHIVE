**Scope:** Research and narrative-craft transfer only

**Project boundary:** Preserve the present-day 1990 village mystery. The former historical Story B mythology remains removed from active development.

## 1. Why this novel matters

*The Ghost Bride* connects ritual practice, family economics, social hierarchy, supernatural rules and an investigation into a death. Its most useful contribution is not its Chinese afterlife cosmology. It is the way the story makes beliefs and rituals consequential within ordinary life.

The novel separates several kinds of information:
- what a character directly experiences;
- what a character believes caused it;
- what another character reports;
- what objects or documents can establish;
- what the supernatural system permits;
- what remains an inference.

That separation is useful when studying a mystery whose evidence includes witness accounts, ritual activity, family secrets, objects and unusual events.

## 2. Conditional techniques worth testing against existing KARUPPI material

| Technique | Possible research use | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| Ritual as social infrastructure | Examine how existing practices affect family status, obligations, inheritance, money, burial, reputation and authority. | Use only rituals and social practices already supported by canon or separately approved local research. |
| Motif / object provenance | Track where an existing object first appears, who owns it, who handles it, what it is believed to mean and what later evidence establishes. | Do not invent new supernatural functions for the painting, anklet, gold sack, drawings or other objects. |
| Separate evidence chains | Maintain distinct records for a human explanation, a witness interpretation and an alleged supernatural event. | Do not connect separate village mysteries unless the Master Story Bible establishes the link. |
| Limited viewpoint | Audit what Rajan, Thangam, Selvi and other established characters know at each stage, and what the audience has been shown. | Do not introduce a new false identity, secret witness or withheld event without canon approval. |
| Rule and consequence ledger | Record the boundaries, costs, permissions and consequences already established for uncanny events. | Do not import the Courts of Hell, ghost-marriage rules, dragon mythology or the novel's afterlife economy. |
| Character power analysis | Study how money, family reputation, gender, age and obligation constrain decisions. | Preserve the 1990 Tamil village context. Do not import colonial Malaya's class system. |
| Cultural specificity audit | Separate local lived practice, witness belief, rumor, official record and fictional invention in research notes. | Do not use Chinese or Peranakan customs as substitutes for Tamil local practices. |

## 3. Practical object / clue ledger

For each already-established clue or object, a future audit can record:

1. First appearance and scene context.
2. Original owner or source.
3. Every person who handles or sees it.
4. The explanation offered by each character.
5. Whether it is direct evidence, hearsay, symbolism or an untested interpretation.
6. What later scene changes its meaning.
7. Whether the final explanation fully accounts for it or leaves it unresolved.

This method is especially useful for continuity and payoff. It does not authorize adding any clue or changing an existing object's role.

## 4. Separate social system from supernatural system

A useful diagnostic from Choo's novel is to ask two different questions:

- What social system governs the characters' ordinary lives?
- What supernatural rules, if any, are already established in the story?

The two may interact, but they should not be collapsed. In KARUPPI, use the latest Master Story Bible to determine the existing village's social arrangements, rituals, restrictions and uncanny events. Do not build a new rule system from this novel.

## 5. Existing KARUPPI elements to preserve

The archive currently records:
- a present-day 1990 isolated Tamil village;
- unnatural darkness beginning around 3 PM;
- unusual restrictions and village behaviors;
- a nightly torch procession entering the forest;
- an abandoned shrine with an eternal fire and faceless statues;
- Arjun's disappearance;
- Rajan's investigation with Thangam;
- Selvi's drawings;
- a monthly gold transaction / gold sack;
- Velanthirayar family secrets;
- Kandasamy's death;
- other unresolved details including a family painting, basement, burned anklet and a post office as a possible communication hub.

This note does not connect these elements or assert a shared cause. The active Master Story Bible remains controlling.

## 6. What not to transfer

Do not import the Chinese ghost-marriage custom, the Plains of the Dead, the novel's ten-court afterlife, Er Lang as a dragon or supernatural investigator, ox-headed demons, paper-offering economy, Fan's body possession, or its qi-transfer mechanics as KARUPPI mythology.

Do not treat the novel as ethnographic evidence for Tamil or Chinese ritual practice. Its copyright page identifies it as fiction, and the author explicitly identifies the Plains of the Dead as an invention and Er Lang's dragon form as an adaptation.

Do not revive the removed historical Karuppi / Maari Story B, the Ravi-Selvaraj triangle, the counter-spell or magical-ring lineage.

**Decision:** Conditional narrative techniques archived. No canon changes. No new mythology. No causal links inferred.
