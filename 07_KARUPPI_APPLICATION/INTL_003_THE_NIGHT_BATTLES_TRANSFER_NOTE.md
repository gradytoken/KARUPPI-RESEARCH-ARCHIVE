## 1. Why this book matters

Ginzburg's central method is to reconstruct a local belief system from records created by an institution whose questions and assumptions often differed from those of the people being questioned. The records are not neutral windows. They are produced through witnesses, hearsay, interrogation, notarial translation, institutional categories and later historical interpretation.

The most useful contribution for a mystery film is not the benandanti belief itself. It is the discipline of separating **event, witness, community explanation, investigator framing and surviving record**.

## 5. Methodological warning

Ginzburg does not prove that every benandanti gathering physically occurred as described. The records are evidence of beliefs, testimony, institutional pressure and social consequences. The translators also explain that the notaries changed Friulian testimony into indirect Italian discourse. A statement in a record must therefore be read with its chain of transmission in view.

For KARUPPI, this suggests a useful standard: a witness account can be dramatically important without being objectively correct, but the story must know which parts are observed, inferred, repeated, misunderstood or manipulated.
