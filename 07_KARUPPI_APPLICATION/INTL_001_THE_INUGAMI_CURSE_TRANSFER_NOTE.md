# INTL-001 Transfer Note — The Inugami Curse

**Source:** Seishi Yokomizo, *The Inugami Curse* (English translation by Yumiko Yamazaki)  
**Classification:** APPLICATION / conditional craft transfer only  
**Canon impact:** None. This note does not add, alter or explain any KARUPPI story element.

## 1. Fit to KARUPPI

This book is not a direct model for Tamil folklore or village ritual. It is a classic inheritance mystery whose “curse” is created and exploited by people. Its value is the way family secrets, symbolic objects, local economics, landscape, staged evidence and identity deception combine to produce an investigation.

The latest KARUPPI Master Story Bible has not been supplied in this research phase. Therefore, all transfers below are conditional. They must not be applied until they can be checked against the actual 1990 village, its geography, the people already in the story and the mystery rules already established.

## 2. Conditional craft transfers

### A. A belief can function as a human weapon without being the supernatural cause

In the novel, Kikuno's curse becomes the symbolic blueprint for murders, but the killings are carried out by people with revenge, greed and protective motives.

**Research application:** When studying KARUPPI's beliefs and rumours, distinguish:
- what people believe happened;
- what a character claims happened;
- what an event physically demonstrates;
- who benefits if the community accepts one explanation;
- whether someone is deliberately using a belief to direct suspicion.

This does not mean that KARUPPI's supernatural elements must be explained away. It means the research should keep human action and supernatural interpretation separate until the source story establishes their relationship.

### B. Family structure can be an investigative mechanism

Sahei's will, unacknowledged children, hidden paternity and competing branches of the family are all part of the murder logic.

**Research application:** For the established Velanthirayar family and any other existing relationships in KARUPPI, build a private research-only relationship map:
- legal relationships;
- biological relationships, only where already established;
- inheritance / ownership relationships;
- who knows which secret;
- who falsely believes something;
- who benefits from a secret remaining hidden.

Do not invent an illegitimate heir, secret paternity or new family branch. First verify what the Master Story Bible already contains.

### C. Evidence can be real but misinterpreted

The handprint comparison in the novel is genuine. The mistake is assuming that a physical match proves the continuity of a person's social identity across several appearances.

**Research application:** For every major clue in KARUPPI, keep separate columns for:
- observed physical fact;
- who observed it;
- when and where it was observed;
- the interpretation a character gives it;
- the alternative explanations available at that point;
- the later evidence that confirms or overturns the interpretation.

This is especially useful for objects, drawings, records, clothing, physical traces and witness identification.

### D. Separate the murderer from the people who manipulate the aftermath

Matsuko commits the killings. Shizuma and Kiyo move bodies, stage symbolic details, hide evidence and redirect suspicion. The resulting case is difficult because the person who performs the concealment has different motives from the murderer.

**Research application:** When auditing existing KARUPPI mysteries, distinguish a possible:
- direct actor;
- witness;
- person who hides or moves something;
- person who spreads a version of events;
- person who unknowingly preserves evidence.

Only use roles already supported by the story bible. Do not create accomplices to solve a gap.

### E. Recurring motifs should change investigative meaning

The axe, koto and chrysanthemum are not repeated as decoration. Each recurrence changes the investigators' model of the murders.

**Research application:** For a recurring object, sound, image or gesture already present in KARUPPI, record what it means at each appearance:
1. first appearance: what does the audience notice?
2. second appearance: what has changed?
3. later appearance: does the evidence confirm, contradict or reframe the earlier interpretation?

Do not add new symbols to manufacture a pattern. The audit is about extracting the function of motifs already in the material.

### F. Landscape should constrain physical events

The lake, sluice, reeds, abandoned house, train station and winter ice determine movement, visibility, concealment and the discovery of bodies.

**Research application:** For KARUPPI's actual forest / mountain setting, map the existing geography as a practical system:
- walking and vehicle routes;
- lines of sight and blind spots;
- sound travel;
- watercourses and crossings;
- access to shrines and buildings;
- weather and seasonal changes;
- how long a person or object could remain hidden;
- what can be reached before or after the village's established restrictions begin.

The exact region and terrain must be verified first. Do not import Nasu's lake, Japanese village transport or mountain conditions.

### G. Community interest becomes believable when it has material consequences

The Inugami family is not merely a rich household. The local economy depends on its business. The townspeople's fascination with the succession is therefore tied to their livelihoods.

**Research application:** Once the KARUPPI village's exact location is confirmed, examine its actual dependencies around 1990: land, employment, transport, credit, food supply, local services, patronage, seasonal work and access to institutions. Ask which households or occupations would be materially affected by the events already in the story.

Do not copy the scale of the Inugami Group. The useful transfer is the causal link between economic dependency and public attention.

### H. Use the calendar as a forensic tool

The novel's timeline links trains, witness sightings, alibis, handprint sessions, murder windows and weather. The investigator's diary is essential to the solution.

**Research application:** Build a chronological ledger for the existing KARUPPI investigation:
- event time versus discovery time;
- who was where;
- who could travel between locations;
- light and weather conditions;
- what the investigator knew at each stage;
- whether a later statement contradicts an earlier one.

This is a structural audit, not permission to alter the story's event order.

### I. A witness's silence must have an emotional or practical cause

Tamayo hesitates to reveal what she suspects because she is emotionally invested in Kiyo and unsure whether her perception is reliable.

**Research application:** When an existing character does not disclose something, record the actual reason supported by the story: fear, loyalty, shame, uncertainty, coercion, self-protection or lack of knowledge. Avoid treating silence as a generic suspense device.

### J. Leave only the right uncertainty unresolved

The final poison in Matsuko's tobacco tray is not explained, even after the central murder case is solved. The main causal structure closes, but one disturbing detail remains.

**Research application:** During the final mystery audit, distinguish:
- the central question that must be answered;
- secondary questions that can remain open;
- clues that have to be explained for fairness;
- details that may remain ambiguous without making the main solution incoherent.

An open ending should feel deliberate and bounded, not like missing work.

## 3. Explicit no-import list

Do not import into KARUPPI:
- the Japanese Inugami possession tradition as a substitute for Tamil guardian-deity belief;
- the fictional Nasu Shrine votive handprint custom;
- the axe / koto / chrysanthemum heirloom set;
- the “yokikotokiku” wordplay;
- Kikuno's curse or the novel's exact revenge pattern;
- the Inugami inheritance condition or a marriage-to-inherit mechanism;
- the Nasu silk-industry town structure;
- the postwar Japanese repatriate identity-switch plot;
- Shizuma / Kiyo's double-identity arrangement;
- the exact murder sequence, decapitation, koto-string staging or upside-down corpse riddle;
- the novel's specific paternity revelations or family configuration.

## 4. Evidence and cultural cautions

The novel's fictional “curse” should not be confused with actual Japanese inugami folk belief. A 1998 study by Masanobu Kagawa describes regionally concentrated possession beliefs in parts of Shikoku and Kyushu, including stigma attached to families thought to transmit inugami. This is a separate cultural subject and must be researched in its own regional context.

Similarly, the novel's Nasu Shrine votive handprint practice is presented by the fictional narrative. This study has not verified it as a widespread Shinto or Japanese military custom.

The source's description of same-sex desire and unconventional relationships uses dated moralising language. Use the plot information about hidden parentage, secrecy and social pressure without reproducing those judgments.

## 5. Questions for a later Master Story Bible audit

Only ask these questions if the existing story material actually raises them:
- Which existing clues are physical observations, and which are characters' interpretations?
- Is the family relationship map clear enough to test motives and knowledge?
- Which existing objects, images, records or sounds recur, and how does each recurrence change meaning?
- Can the protagonist's movement and the timing of the investigation be reconstructed without contradictions?
- Which existing characters could know about the same event from different positions?
- Does the local economy make the village's interest in the existing family or mystery believable?
- Do the current geography, weather and access constraints support the movement described?
- Which mystery question must be answered, and which can remain open?

## 6. Transfer verdict

**High value:** mystery architecture, timeline reconstruction, evidence misinterpretation, layered identity, separation of culprit from evidence-mover, recurring motif logic.

**Medium value:** community economics, family secrecy and landscape as a physical constraint. These require region-specific validation.

**Low direct-transfer value:** Japanese ritual custom, the Inugami possession tradition, the novel's heirlooms, family curse, and postwar repatriation plot.

**Final rule:** *The Inugami Curse* is a model for how to construct and solve a dense human mystery, not a sourcebook for KARUPPI's Tamil folklore.
