# Horror Grammar: Working Principles

These are craft hypotheses synthesized from prior film / book study. They are not universal laws. Test them against each source and against the actual dramatic problem.

## 1. Cause ambiguity, event clarity

The audience may not know why something happened, but should understand what happened, who experienced it, and what changed.

Confusing action is not the same as productive mystery.

## 2. Withholding is not escalation

Withholding information creates a question. It does not automatically increase tension.

An escalation should do at least one of the following:
- Change what the protagonist can safely do.
- Increase exposure or risk.
- Damage or transform a relationship.
- Force a decision.
- Make an earlier interpretation untenable.
- Create a cost that persists.

## 3. Protective action can backfire

A character acts to protect someone, preserve a secret, or obey a rule. The action may create a new danger or remove another person's options. The reversal should arise from established character motives and story rules.

## 4. Relationship as an attack surface

Fear becomes intimate when the mystery changes trust, obligation, dependency, secrecy, or the ability to protect someone. Relationships should not exist only to deliver exposition.

## 5. Information control

Track:
- What the audience knows.
- What each character knows.
- What each character believes.
- What is objectively established.
- What is still only rumour or interpretation.

Do not let the script accidentally treat a character's belief as a confirmed fact.

## 6. Sensory grammar

A sound, image, smell, or physical sensation should have an established baseline before its meaning changes. Repetition can create anticipation. Variation can create dread.

## 7. Evidence must alter the investigation

A clue should change a belief, decision, route, risk, relationship, or available action. A strange-looking object that changes nothing is set dressing, not functioning evidence.

## 8. Rules need observable consequences

A local taboo or restriction becomes dramatic when it changes behaviour and when breaking it has a visible or credible consequence. A lore speech alone does not establish a rule.

## 9. Helpers must not erase protagonist agency

A knowledgeable helper can mislead, assist, refuse, misunderstand, or pay a cost. If the helper explains and solves the central mystery for the protagonist, the investigation loses force.

## 10. Explanation has a cost

A final explanation should answer the questions the story promised to answer, but preserve only ambiguity that is deliberate and earned. An explanation dump can flatten the emotional experience.

## 11. Audit every mystery thread

For each thread, record:
- First appearance.
- Why the audience should care.
- Evidence added.
- Consequence for a character.
- Connection to the central question.
- Payoff or deliberate non-payoff.

Too many active threads can create noise rather than depth.

## 12. Review test

For every suspense beat ask: "What has changed by the end of this beat?" If the answer is only "the audience knows less," the beat may be atmospheric but is not yet dramatic escalation.
