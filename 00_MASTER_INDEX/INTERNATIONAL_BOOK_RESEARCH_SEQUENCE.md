# International Book Research Sequence

**Programme:** Five selected books from outside the Tamil-language research corpus
**Purpose:** Expand KARUPPI research through mystery architecture, community folklore, historical belief, unusual experience testimony and ritual / social systems.
**Method:** One book at a time. Each book receives a full or clearly bounded study, source / evidence distinctions, conditional KARUPPI transfer note and GitHub update.
**Canon status:** Research only. No book creates KARUPPI canon.

## Selection logic

The five titles deliberately cover different research functions. They are not simply five more supernatural novels.

## INTL-001 — The Inugami Curse

**Study:** 02_BOOK_STUDIES/INTL_001_The_Inugami_Curse.md
**Transfer note:** 07_KARUPPI_APPLICATION/INTL_001_THE_INUGAMI_CURSE_TRANSFER_NOTE.md

Completed from the supplied 246-page English PDF. Translation by Yumiko Yamazaki. The supplied book's copyright page identifies the original Japanese edition as a 1972 Kadokawa publication; the English edition is listed by the Japan Foundation catalogue as a 2020 Pushkin Press edition.

The novel is a family-inheritance detective mystery with a curse structure, not a direct ethnographic source or a conventional supernatural horror novel. Its strongest research contributions are:
- a will that actively creates the motive field;
- hidden family relationships as part of the puzzle;
- genuine physical evidence misinterpreted through a false identity assumption;
- the separation of the direct murderer from the people who conceal / restage evidence;
- a setting whose landscape and economy constrain the crime;
- an unresolved final poison detail after the central solution closes.

The study separately distinguishes the novel's fictional Kikuno curse from actual Japanese inugami possession folklore. No KARUPPI canon changes were made.

### INTL-002 — Folk
**Zoe Gilbert. Completed.** Full study archived in `02_BOOK_STUDIES/INTL_002_Folk.md`, with conditional transfer note in `07_KARUPPI_APPLICATION/INTL_002_FOLK_TRANSFER_NOTE.md`.

The supplied 155-page 2018 Bloomsbury electronic edition was studied across all 15 stories. The study maps the Neverness geography, recurring characters, shared customs, social memory, limited witness knowledge, sensory motifs, seasonal rhythm and the circular relationship between Prick Song and Tether. The copyright page and acknowledgements were checked. Eight stories are identified in the book's end matter as having appeared previously in slightly different forms. The source is treated as fiction, not ethnography. No KARUPPI canon changes were made.

### INTL-003 — The Night Battles
**Carlo Ginzburg. Completed.** Full study archived in `02_BOOK_STUDIES/INTL_003_The_Night_Battles.md`, with conditional transfer note in `07_KARUPPI_APPLICATION/INTL_003_THE_NIGHT_BATTLES_TRANSFER_NOTE.md`.

The supplied English edition of *I Benandanti* was reviewed through its four chapters, prefaces, translators' note, appendix, notes and index. The study follows the benandanti from early accounts of harvest-protecting night battles and journeys with the dead through later testimony increasingly entangled with demonological sabbat categories. It distinguishes reported experience, hearsay, interrogation, notarial mediation and historical inference. It also records Ginzburg's explicit qualification that the sources do not establish that every reported night gathering physically occurred. No KARUPPI canon changes were made.

### INTL-004 — The Terror That Comes in the Night
**David J. Hufford. Completed.** Full study archived in `02_BOOK_STUDIES/INTL_004_The_Terror_That_Comes_in_the_Night.md`, with conditional transfer note in `07_KARUPPI_APPLICATION/INTL_004_THE_TERROR_THAT_COMES_IN_THE_NIGHT_TRANSFER_NOTE.md`.

The supplied 235-page 1982 University of Pennsylvania Press edition was reviewed through the introduction, five chapters, conclusion, appendix, bibliography and feature index. The study maps Hufford's experience-centered method, the Cultural Source and Experiential Source hypotheses, Newfoundland accounts, questionnaire design, the four primary features of the Old Hag, secondary experiential features, his critique of sleep-related explanations, and cross-cultural examples involving hauntings, witch-riding, vampires, Inuit augumangia / ukomiarik and Philippine aswang. It distinguishes experience from cultural model and causal explanation. The author's 15-percent prevalence estimate is qualified as a 1982 finding, not a current statistic. No KARUPPI canon changes were made.
