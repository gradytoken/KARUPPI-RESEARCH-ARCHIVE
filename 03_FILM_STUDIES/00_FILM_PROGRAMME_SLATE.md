## 6. Research method

Each pair should include, where evidence permits:

- Source record: edition, runtime, language, production context and viewing / research limitations.
- Scene-level progression and timestamp anchors with source / version confidence.
- Mystery architecture, clue design, evidence chains, reveal structure and narrative consequences.
- Character motivation, psychology, social relations, material pressures and moral costs.
- Folklore / literary source research, with a strict distinction between documented cultural material and fictional diegetic lore.
- Visual grammar: camera distance, movement, blocking, composition, production design, geography and thresholds.
- Sound grammar: voice, music, silence, environmental sound, recurring motifs and sonic perspective.
- Audience pulse, including Reddit discussions where available. Treat social posts as self-selected anecdotes, never representative polls.
- Comparison with CMP-002's seven-layer model: **World → Community → Individual → Event → Evidence → Interpretation → Consequence**, plus Material Economy, Rule and Ritual, Time and Memory, Power and Voice.
- Transferable filmmaking mechanisms separated from culturally specific beliefs, symbols and practices.
- Failure modes, limitations, unresolved questions and a pair verdict.

## 7. Evidence integrity

Use the labels SOURCE, ANALYSIS, AUDIENCE PULSE, CULTURAL BOUNDARY, TRANSFERABLE MECHANISM, and OPEN / NEEDS VERIFICATION.

Do not claim a first-person frame-by-frame viewing if the research is source-led. Do not convert approximate scene windows into exact timecodes. If the edition or runtime differs, record the difference. Folklore claims require independent cultural / historical sources and must not be inferred from a fictional film alone.

## 8. Canon boundary

All film and series studies are research only. Do not create story explanations, import source-film mythology into KARUPPI, alter any character or event, or make plot proposals unless the user explicitly changes the scope.
