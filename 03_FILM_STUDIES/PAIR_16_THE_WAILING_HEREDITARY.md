# PAIR 16 — *The Wailing* (2016) + *Hereditary* (2018)

**Status:** Modern-extension comparative dossier completed, source-led  
**Research mode:** Film research only  
**Spoiler note:** The dossier discusses full mystery and ending architecture.  
**Canon boundary:** No KARUPPI story explanation, mythology transfer, plot proposal, character mapping or canon change is authorised.

---

## 1. Research question

How do contemporary horror films turn inherited or communal uncertainty into a system of escalating evidence, and what happens when the audience cannot easily distinguish human interpretation from supernatural agency?

The pair studies two very different modern horror architectures:

- Na Hong-jin's *The Wailing* begins with unexplained violence in a rural Korean community. Rumour, police procedure, witness accounts, a sick child, a stranger, a shamanic ritual and Christian imagery create several competing explanations.
- Ari Aster's *Hereditary* begins with a family's bereavement and gradually exposes an inherited occult design. Domestic space, miniatures, family memory, visual motifs and sound make the family home feel both intimate and controlled by forces outside the household.

**Executive finding:** *The Wailing* distributes uncertainty across a community and several competing authorities. *Hereditary* concentrates uncertainty inside a family whose private history is slowly revealed as part of a larger design. The first builds a crisis of interpretation. The second turns apparently private grief into evidence of a predetermined structure.

Their endings handle uncertainty differently. *The Wailing* refuses a comfortable explanatory closure and leaves the viewer to assess contradictory signs. *Hereditary* eventually makes its occult system more explicit, which retrospectively reorganises much of the preceding imagery. This is not a simple contrast between ambiguity and certainty: both films manipulate what can be known, but they allocate certainty differently.

## 3. Source and edition records

### 3.1 *The Wailing* (2016)

**SOURCE**
- Original Korean title: *Gokseong* (곡성).
- Written and directed by Na Hong-jin.
- South Korea / United States production, released in 2016.
- Principal cast includes Kwak Do-won as Jong-goo, Hwang Jung-min as Il-gwang, Chun Woo-hee as the mysterious woman, Jun Kunimura as the Stranger and Kim Hwan-hee as Hyo-jin.
- The Cannes Film Festival and Busan International Film Festival list a 156-minute runtime. The US distributor Well Go USA lists 157 minutes. Treat 156–157 minutes as a catalogue / transfer discrepancy until a specific edition is named.
- The official Cannes synopsis frames the film around a stranger's arrival, rumours, unexplained deaths and escalating suspicion in a rural village.
- Na Hong-jin has said he set the story in Gokseong because he knew the area through his grandmother's hometown and wanted the actual landscape to shape the film's visual and emotional atmosphere.
- The director has discussed combining Korean shamanism and Catholic / Christian material rather than repeating a purely Catholic occult template.

**Edition caution**
Use the 156-minute festival record as a working reference. The 157-minute distributor listing may reflect transfer or catalogue convention. Record the runtime and opening / ending credits of the actual copy before timing.

### 3.2 *Hereditary* (2018)

**SOURCE**
- Written and directed by Ari Aster; feature debut.
- United States production, released in 2018.
- Principal cast includes Toni Collette as Annie Graham, Gabriel Byrne as Steve, Alex Wolff as Peter, Milly Shapiro as Charlie and Ann Dowd as Joan.
- A24 lists a 127-minute runtime. BFI lists approximately 127 minutes (127 minutes 15 seconds in its record); AFI and some catalogues list 126 minutes. Use the actual copy's credits and runtime.
- Cinematography by Pawel Pogorzelski; editing by Jennifer Lame and Lucian Johnston; music by Colin Stetson.
- BFI's review identifies the opening miniature / dollhouse movement as a formal key: the camera moves from a model of the house into the apparent full-scale domestic environment.
- Aster has explained that the house was built from scratch in order to make camera movement and longer sequenced shots possible. The miniature-to-real-room transition used a separately filmed room plate incorporated into the miniature shot.
- Aster has described the film as centred on family, grief and the inner lives of its characters, rather than approaching horror as a sequence of external shocks.

**Edition caution**
A24's 127-minute record is a useful baseline. BFI's more precise 127:15 and the 126-minute catalogue listings should be retained rather than silently normalised.

## 4. Cultural, religious and psychological boundaries

### 4.1 *The Wailing*: several religious systems within one fictional crisis

**SOURCE**
Na Hong-jin stated that he wanted to place Korean shamanism alongside Catholicism and other religious material because he did not want to repeat an exclusively Catholic occult template. He has also described visiting rituals in several Asian countries while researching the film. In an interview with The Playlist, he said the music in the major shamanic sequence was performed by actual shamans and that the actions were based on an exorcism ritual he had witnessed.

A 2025 scholarly chapter by Nikki J. Y. Lee analyses *The Wailing* within contemporary Korean occult cinema, with particular attention to sound, the movement between belief and disbelief, and the sensory power of ritual sequences.

**ANALYSIS**
The film does not make one religious vocabulary the neutral authority that explains all the others. The police, villagers, shaman, Christian priest, anonymous witness and family members each operate with incomplete knowledge. The viewer is moved among these frameworks rather than receiving a single stable interpretive key.

The ritual sequence derives much of its force from performance, percussion, duration, editing and the interaction of simultaneous actions. Its sonic scale creates a felt contest between explanations before the viewer can reliably decide what the ritual proves.

**CULTURAL BOUNDARY**
Do not treat the film as a comprehensive or authoritative account of Korean shamanism. Na Hong-jin's remarks concern his production research and the particular ritual performance staged for the film. Korean shamanic practice is diverse, regionally and historically varied, and cannot be reduced to a single cinematic sequence. Do not equate all Korean shamans with horror-film exorcists. The Stranger, the anonymous woman and the film's supernatural rules are fictional constructions.

### 4.2 *Hereditary*: fictional occult mechanics and family trauma

**SOURCE**
A24's synopsis frames the film around a family trying to escape an inherited fate after the death of its matriarch. BFI's critical analysis describes the dollhouse and miniature motifs as a way of merging psychological realism with a sense that the family is being arranged by an outside design. Film Comment's interview with Aster discusses the film's family focus and its relation to grief and inherited trauma.

A 2026 *Horror Studies* article by Marcus Nicholls, Jonathon Crewe and Dennis Olsen offers a scholarly reading of *Hereditary* through anxieties about ageing and dementia. This is a specific critical interpretation, not a settled account of authorial intent or the film's only meaning.

**ANALYSIS**
The film places grief, family secrecy, inherited behaviour and occult conspiracy in the same narrative field. Early images invite psychological readings. Later information reframes some of those images as parts of a supernatural design. The viewer must revise earlier interpretations without assuming that the film has offered a realistic account of psychiatric illness.

**CULTURAL BOUNDARY**
Paimon, the cult's rules and the film's occult genealogy are fictional diegetic lore. They are not documentation of an actual religious community, historical occult organisation or established ritual tradition. Do not treat the film's depiction of grief, dissociation, trauma, ageing or mental illness as clinical evidence. Do not conflate real diagnoses with violence or supernatural possession.

## 5. Mystery architecture and information control

### 5.1 *The Wailing*

**ANALYSIS**
- The first wave of violence is filtered through police procedure and local rumour. The initial explanation is mundane and provisional.
- A witness's account and the appearance of the Stranger transform a local crime problem into a crisis of belief.
- Jong-goo's daughter's illness makes the investigation personal and reduces his ability to maintain professional distance.
- Il-gwang's ritual introduces a second investigative method, but the film does not make the ritual's meaning immediately transparent.
- The anonymous woman introduces another source of warning and another possible interpretation of the Stranger.
- The viewer is repeatedly asked to choose between explanations before possessing enough evidence to confirm one.
- The final sequence turns a seemingly practical instruction into a test of trust. The cost of choosing incorrectly is made immediate.

**Mystery type**
A multi-authority epistemic mystery. The central problem is not merely discovering which entity is responsible. It is deciding whose account can be trusted, what counts as evidence and how action should be taken when every authority is compromised by uncertainty.

### 5.2 *Hereditary*

**ANALYSIS**
- The obituary and funeral establish the matriarch's death while exposing unresolved family history.
- Annie's miniatures make domestic memory visible as a constructed object. The camera's movement between miniature and full-scale space quietly establishes the film's central visual problem.
- Strange details are distributed across family objects, rooms, drawings, photographs and repeated symbols.
- The family initially interprets events through grief, mental distress, interpersonal blame and household conflict.
- Joan's arrival creates an alternative channel of explanation, but her authority is not neutral.
- The later revelations connect the family's past to a more organised occult plan.
- The ending gives the viewer a stronger account of the cult's purpose, but the emotional consequences of the family's destruction remain central.

**Mystery type**
An inherited-design mystery. The audience is first encouraged to treat details as expressions of family trauma, then asked to recognise how those details fit a hidden system.

### 5.3 Pair comparison

*The Wailing* multiplies explanations and keeps them in conflict. *Hereditary* distributes clues across apparently separate domestic events, then compresses them into a coherent occult design. One is a contest between interpretations. The other is a gradual recognition of the pattern that has been controlling the characters.

## 6. Character, authority and moral pressure

### *The Wailing*
- Jong-goo's role as a police officer is complicated by fear for his daughter. His investigation becomes increasingly personal.
- Hyo-jin is not simply a symptom or supernatural object. Her suffering drives the family's decisions and exposes the limits of the adults' explanations.
- Il-gwang's professional ritual authority is powerful, but the film complicates how the viewer should assess his claims.
- The Stranger is treated through rumour, xenophobic suspicion and supernatural fear. The film's uncertainty makes the consequences of accusation part of its moral problem.
- The anonymous woman has knowledge but not institutional authority. The viewer must evaluate her warnings without a convenient verification procedure.

### *Hereditary*
- Annie's work with miniatures externalises a need to organise and control family history. The form becomes increasingly disturbing as her control weakens.
- Steve attempts to preserve ordinary family functioning, but his scepticism does not resolve the crisis.
- Peter is caught between guilt, fear and a family history he cannot fully understand.
- Charlie's position within the family is shaped by the grandmother's attention and the adults' interpretations of her behaviour.
- Joan's emotional availability makes her persuasive before the audience understands the full purpose of her presence.

**ANALYSIS**
Both films show that authority is not simply a matter of title. It emerges through trust, personal need, performance, family connection and the ability to make an explanation feel actionable.

## 7. Visual grammar

### 7.1 *The Wailing*
- The actual Gokseong landscape gives the village, ridges, roads and surrounding nature a specific spatial identity.
- The director explicitly described location as part of the film's concept and light, not just a backdrop.
- Wide landscape views place people inside a larger environment that seems to contain them.
- Domestic rooms and local buildings provide recurring spaces where rumour and evidence are exchanged.
- The shamanic ritual sequence cuts between parallel actions, creating visual competition between the two ritual sites.
- The film alternates between ordinary rural detail and sudden disturbing images. The difference in scale helps maintain uncertainty about whether an event is part of daily life, rumour or supernatural escalation.

### 7.2 *Hereditary*
- The miniature house makes the home a model that can be observed, arranged and controlled.
- The opening movement from miniature to full-sized room establishes the film's visual thesis before the audience knows what it means.
- Aster had the house built to allow longer camera movements and shots that could travel through rooms without conventional coverage.
- Doors, corridors, stairways, attic spaces and treehouse geography give the domestic environment a system of thresholds.
- Repeated motifs become more legible as the family mystery develops.
- The late movement toward a composed ritual tableau contrasts with the earlier naturalistic family drama.

## 8. Sound grammar

### *The Wailing*
**SOURCE**
Na Hong-jin discussed the shamanic sequence's drum-driven music and the use of ritual performers. Nikki J. Y. Lee's 2025 chapter specifically studies how sound in contemporary Korean occult cinema produces sensory momentum and stages belief and disbelief.

**ANALYSIS**
Sound does not merely signal that a supernatural event has begun. It structures the viewer's experience of competing ritual actions. Rhythm, volume, repeated vocal and percussive patterns and the editing of parallel spaces produce a contest that is felt before it is intellectually resolved.

### *Hereditary*
**SOURCE**
Beth Kattelman's 2022 *Horror Studies* article studies the film's soundscape, including Colin Stetson's unusual reed-instrument techniques, vocalisation, percussive key sounds, silence, infrasound and subliminal or corporeal sound. Stetson has separately discussed avoiding conventional string-and-synth horror scoring and developing sounds through clarinets, saxophones, his own voice and unusual acoustic techniques.

**ANALYSIS**
The score can conceal itself within the film's texture rather than always announcing a conventional musical theme. Sound becomes a channel for information the viewer may feel before consciously identifying it. The contrast between intimate domestic speech and physically unsettling sonic material helps the film keep psychological and supernatural interpretations in play.

## 9. Sequence anchors

These are chronological navigation anchors, not exact timestamps.

### *The Wailing*
1. **Opening report and village disturbance:** the film establishes unexplained violence and a police response.
2. **The Stranger enters local rumour:** suspicion becomes a social force before it becomes verified evidence.
3. **Jong-goo's family crisis:** the daughter's illness makes the investigation personal.
4. **The shaman arrives:** another authority enters the case, and ritual becomes a competing method of explanation.
5. **The anonymous woman's warnings:** the viewer encounters an alternative interpretation with uncertain credibility.
6. **The ritual confrontation:** sound and parallel action create an intense sensory contest.
7. **The final warning and decision:** the story turns interpretation into an irreversible action.
8. **The ending:** the viewer is left to assess what the film has shown, what the characters believed and where the evidence remains incomplete.

### *Hereditary*
1. **Obituary and miniature house:** family history and scale are established as formal motifs.
2. **Funeral and early family conflict:** grief becomes a field of competing responses.
3. **The accident and its aftermath:** trauma changes the family's behaviour and the viewer's expectations.
4. **Annie's miniatures and family discoveries:** visual details begin to connect with the family past.
5. **Joan's intervention:** a new explanatory authority enters the family.
6. **The escalation of apparitions and conflict:** psychological and supernatural readings become harder to separate.
7. **The revelation of an organised occult design:** earlier images are reinterpreted.
8. **The final tableau:** the hidden system's intended outcome becomes visible.

## 10. CMP-002 model comparison

The archive's seven-layer model is **World → Community → Individual → Event → Evidence → Interpretation → Consequence**, with Material Economy, Rule and Ritual, Time and Memory, Power and Voice as cross-cutting lenses.

| Layer | *The Wailing* | *Hereditary* |
|---|---|---|
| World | Rural Korean village, roads, landscape and religious plurality | Suburban home, school, miniature rooms and family history |
| Community | Police, villagers, family, shamanic and Christian figures | Family, friends, occult group and inherited kinship network |
| Individual | Jong-goo, Hyo-jin, Il-gwang, the Stranger and anonymous witness | Annie, Steve, Peter, Charlie, Ellen and Joan |
| Event | Unexplained deaths, illness, ritual and escalating suspicion | Bereavement, family trauma, revelations and occult orchestration |
| Evidence | Rumour, witness testimony, police findings, ritual and visible phenomena | Miniatures, symbols, drawings, photographs, behaviour and testimony |
| Interpretation | Several competing religious and practical explanations | Psychological, familial and occult explanations progressively converge |
| Consequence | A decision made under uncertainty produces irreversible loss | The family is destroyed by a plan it could not fully recognise or stop |

**Cross-cutting observations**
- **Material Economy:** the rural community's work and institutional limits contrast with the domestic comfort and property of the Graham family.
- **Rule and Ritual:** several religious authorities and a contested ritual in one; hidden cult rules and arranged ceremony in the other.
- **Time and Memory:** rumour and inherited local memory versus family genealogy and intergenerational design.
- **Power and Voice:** institutional authority, social suspicion and ritual expertise versus the hidden authority of the older generation and cult.

## 11. Transferable mechanisms

These are abstract craft observations only.

1. **Distribute explanations across people with different kinds of authority.** The viewer should understand why each person believes what they believe, without treating every explanation as equally reliable.
2. **Make the setting alter the investigation.** Location should determine who can witness events, how rumours travel, what routes are available and what evidence can be recovered.
3. **Let sound carry evidence or conflict.** A ritual's sound can communicate more than a spoken explanation, while an unusual musical texture can foreshadow information the audience has not yet consciously processed.
4. **Make a repeated visual object change meaning.** A miniature, photograph, room or landscape can first appear practical and later become part of a larger pattern.
5. **Separate family trauma from supernatural causality.** The viewer can be invited to consider both without implying that mental illness or grief is itself supernatural evil.
6. **Use revelation to reorganise earlier evidence.** The audience should be able to look back and recognise a pattern, not merely receive new rules at the end.
7. **Preserve moral uncertainty when factual certainty is unavailable.** A character can be wrong in a way that remains understandable and consequential.

## 12. Failure modes and limits

- Do not equate Korean shamanic practice with a single horror-film ritual.
- Do not treat Na Hong-jin's interview about the film's ritual research as proof that every action in the sequence is a universal Korean practice.
- Do not reduce the Stranger to a real-world ethnic or national stereotype.
- Do not treat the film's religious imagery as a theological statement made on behalf of every represented tradition.
- Do not treat Paimon or the cult in *Hereditary* as a historical occult system.
- Do not use the film's mental-health imagery as clinical evidence or equate a diagnosis with violence.
- Do not reduce *Hereditary* to a puzzle about occult symbols and ignore its family drama.
- Do not claim exact timecodes without an edition-matched subtitle record.
- Do not interpret the two films' very different endings as a universal rule about ambiguity or closure.

## 14. Pair verdict

*The Wailing* makes an entire community participate in the construction of an explanation. Its mystery becomes dangerous because people must act before the competing accounts can be reconciled. *Hereditary* makes a family live inside an explanation it cannot see until the hidden design is almost complete.

Together they offer two strong modern horror models: communal epistemic instability and domestic inherited conspiracy. Their formal differences are important. The first is a long, sprawling contest between interpretations. The second is a tightly controlled spatial and sonic design that turns family history into an increasingly legible trap.
